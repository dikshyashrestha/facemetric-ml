# Final comparison: deep metric learning for LFW face verification

All numbers come from the saved View 2 results of each model folder and are regenerated by
`python Comparison_Final/compare_methods.py` (no training, no re-tuning). The script re-verified
every result (204 checks, 0 failures; see `verification_report.md`).

## Setup shared by all methods

- Shared modified ResNet-18 + BN-Dropout-FC-BN 512-D embedding, random initialization, AdamW
  (weight decay 5e-4) with cosine decay, 112×112 deep-funneled images, flip-only augmentation,
  test-time features = original + flipped embedding, L2-normalized.
- Reduced cross-fold scheme (PDF §4.2): 5 fresh models per method; run *j* trains on 8 folds and
  scores folds 2*j*−1 and 2*j*. Each fold's threshold is chosen on the other nine folds'
  out-of-fold scores (5,400 pairs). All methods scored the identical 6,000 official pairs.
- Hyperparameters come from each owner's View 1 tuning (rank-1 row, verified); View 2 was not
  used for any selection.
- One seed (42) per run. **The ± values are the sample SD across the 10 folds of that single
  seed. They describe fold-to-fold variation, not variation across random seeds.**

## Main results (cosine similarity; `comparison_table.md`)

| Method | Protocol | Accuracy % (mean ± SD) | AUC | EER | TAR@FAR=1% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triplet, semi-hard (α=0.2) | Unrestricted | **86.47 ± 1.62** | **0.936** | **0.134** | 0.428 |
| Triplet, batch-hard (α=0.3) | Unrestricted | 85.40 ± 1.90 | 0.932 | 0.145 | 0.428 |
| Softmax + Center Loss (λ=0.01) | Unrestricted | 80.68 ± 2.50 | 0.888 | 0.191 | 0.254 |
| Softmax baseline | Unrestricted | 78.27 ± 2.35 | 0.868 | 0.221 | 0.312 |
| SphereFace (m=3, annealed) | Unrestricted | 78.08 ± 3.02 | 0.869 | 0.215 | 0.303 |
| ArcFace (s=30, m=0.3) | Unrestricted | 71.77 ± 1.81 | 0.788 | 0.284 | 0.181 |
| Siamese (margin 1.25) | Unrestricted | 66.48 ± 3.97 | 0.713 | 0.335 | 0.092 |
| Siamese (margin 1.25) | **Image-restricted** | 63.23 ± 2.19 | 0.676 | 0.365 | 0.052 |

Squared Euclidean distance gives identical accuracy, AUC, EER and TAR for Siamese and Triplet,
as expected for unit vectors (d² = 2 − 2·cos, confirmed to within 5e-7).

## Findings

1. **Triplet loss performed best; the Siamese network performed worst.** Semi-hard triplet
   was better than every other method on all 10 folds except batch-hard triplet (8/10 folds).
   Its accuracy advantage over the next family (Center Loss) is 5.8 points.
2. **Mining strategy (semi-hard vs batch-hard): small but consistent difference.** Semi-hard
   was 1.07 points more accurate (8/10 folds, Holm-adjusted paired p ≈ 0.02), with essentially
   the same TAR@FAR=1% (0.428 vs 0.428). Neither triplet configuration had plateaued on View 1
   at 30 epochs, so both may be under-trained.
3. **Restricted vs unrestricted Siamese:** the extra identity-derived pairs added 3.25 points
   (8/10 folds better, one tie; Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon p ≈ 0.018) and raised AUC from 0.676 to
   0.713. Both variants used the same loss, margin, number of pairs per epoch (4,800) and epochs,
   so the gain comes from label information (fresh, more varied pairs), not from the loss. Both
   remain far below triplet loss, which uses the same identity labels.
4. **Center Loss on top of softmax:** +2.42 points (9/10 folds, Holm-adjusted t-test p ≈ 0.001)
   and higher AUC (0.888 vs 0.868). The View 1 statistics support the compactness effect: mean
   intra-class distance to centers fell from 19.5 (softmax) to 10.6 (λ=0.01), while inter-center
   distance changed less (26.5 → 23.4). However, softmax has the better TAR at FAR=1%
   (0.312 vs 0.254), so the gain is in the middle of the ROC, not at strict operating points.
5. **Angular margins did not beat plain softmax here.** SphereFace equals the softmax baseline
   (78.08 vs 78.27, AUC 0.869 vs 0.868). ArcFace is 6.5 points below it (see unresolved item 1).
6. **Classification losses vs pair/triplet losses:** on this small, long-tailed training set
   (≈7,000–7,800 images of ≈1,300 classes per run), triplet loss used the data most efficiently.
   This differs from the large-scale literature, where angular-margin classifiers dominate; the
   gap to published 99%+ results reflects training on ~1% of the data those papers used.

## Ablations (View 1, as recorded; `view1_ablations.md`)

View 1 accuracy uses the best threshold on the 1,000 validation pairs (tuning metric, optimistic).

- **Siamese margin:** accuracy rose monotonically with margin for both variants
  (restricted 0.566 → 0.588 → 0.604 → 0.644 for m = 0.5 → 1.25). The selected margin is the
  edge of the searched range, so a larger margin might be better.
- **Triplet:** lr 1e-4 beat 3e-4 for every margin and both mining methods; margin mattered less
  (batch-hard 0.810–0.820, semi-hard 0.822–0.841).
- **Center Loss λ:** 0.001 → 0.800, 0.003 → 0.819, 0.01 → 0.824, 0.03 → 0.725 (softmax 0.778).
  Too much weight on compactness hurts.
- **SphereFace:** m=2/3/4 with annealing 0.798/0.803/0.790; m=1 (normalized softmax) 0.792;
  **m=4 without annealing 0.585**, confirming annealing is needed for convergence.
- **ArcFace:** s=64 diverged or stopped early (0.56–0.58); s=16/30 gave 0.64–0.68. The margin
  ablation at s=30 gave m=0 → 0.686, m=0.3 → 0.684 (selected), m=0.5 → 0.679, m=0.2 → 0.646.

## Statistical comparison (`statistical_tests.md`)

Paired t-tests and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests over the 10 fold accuracies, Holm-corrected within
each family. Semi-hard triplet differs from every other method at Holm-adjusted p < 0.02 under
both tests. Interpret with care:
- The two folds scored by one run share a model, so the 10 paired differences are not independent;
  p-values are likely optimistic. With only 5 independent models per method the effective sample
  size is smaller than 10.
- With n = 10 the smallest attainable two-sided Wilcoxon p is ≈ 0.002.
- Differences such as SphereFace vs softmax (−0.2 points) are well inside one fold SD and should
  not be treated as real. One seed means seed-to-seed variability is unknown.

## Training cost (`training_cost.md`)

Recorded training time per method (5 runs): Softmax 6.6 h, Center 6.6 h, SphereFace 6.5 h,
Triplet 9.5–10.0 h, Siamese 24.6–25.3 h, ArcFace 4.2 h. **These are not comparable** as a
cost ranking: the runs used different machines (Siamese: 8 GB Apple M1; Triplet/Center/SphereFace:
other Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 26, chip not recorded; ArcFace: CUDA Tesla T4), different PyTorch
versions (2.11–2.14) and different work per epoch. For example, a Siamese epoch processes 9,600
images and a Triplet epoch 10,800, yet Siamese epochs took ~10 min and Triplet ~4 min, which points
to hardware rather than the loss. Memory is also measured differently: MPS values (3.4–5.5 GB) are
the largest per-batch sample of driver memory, the T4 value (2.0 GB) is PyTorch's true tensor peak.

## Unresolved items and discrepancies (reported, not changed)

1. **ArcFace is unexpectedly weak.** Its own m=0 ablation (0.686 on View 1) is far below
   SphereFace's normalized-softmax m=1 run (0.792) and the plain softmax (0.778), although all
   three are softmax-style classifiers on the same backbone and classes. The margin is therefore
   not the cause; the ArcFace pipeline differs in other ways (P×K identity sampling 8×4 with
   9,600 images/epoch instead of shuffled batches of 64, feature normalization with s=30, CUDA
   T4 and PyTorch 2.11). The ArcFace owner should check this before the report draws conclusions
   about additive margins.
2. **Different epoch policies.** ArcFace and Triplet used a View 1 plateau rule (ArcFace trained
   28 epochs on View 2); the other methods used a fixed 30-epoch budget. All are View 1 decisions,
   but the policy differs and should be stated.
3. **Learning rate was tuned only for Triplet and ArcFace** (1e-4 vs 3e-4). Siamese, Softmax,
   Center Loss and SphereFace used lr 1e-4 only, although PDF §3 asks for per-method lr tuning.
4. **Batch composition differs:** Siamese 16 balanced pairs; Triplet P×K = 8×4 + 4 singletons
   (PDF suggests P = 32, reduced for memory); Softmax/Center/SphereFace 64 shuffled images;
   ArcFace P×K = 8×4.
5. **One seed** for all methods (PDF asks for ≥3 if compute permits): must be disclosed.
6. **LFWPeople compliance** is still open (README): the data come from the torchvision-linked
   Kaggle mirror with MD5-verified protocols, but the loaders do not use
   `torchvision.datasets.LFWPeople`.
7. **TAR@FAR=1%** rests on the 30 highest-scoring impostor pairs of 3,000 and is noisy.
8. **Not produced here:** the confident-error face gallery (pair lists are in each model's
   `confident_errors.csv`; images stay local), embedding visualizations, and per-method score
   histograms (each model folder has `score_histogram.png`).
